# Air Fried Herb Chicken

A simple, Mediterranean-inspired chicken dish featuring dried herbs and spices, prepared in an air fryer for a quick weeknight meal.

Source: [Fit Men Cook](https://fitmencook.com/recipes/air-fried-herb-chicken/)

**Servings:** 4
**Prep time:** 20 minutes
**Cook time:** 20 minutes
**Total time:** 40 minutes

## Ingredients

**Protein:**
- 1½ lb lean chicken thighs (or chicken breasts)

**Marinade:**
- 1 tablespoon olive oil mayonnaise
- 2 teaspoons dried thyme leaves
- 1 teaspoon cumin
- 1 teaspoon ground oregano powder
- 1 teaspoon fennel powder
- Pinch of sea salt and fresh cracked pepper

## Instructions

1. Pat chicken dry with paper towels and trim visible fat. Combine chicken with all marinade ingredients in a bowl.
2. Cover and marinate for 20 minutes at room temperature, or refrigerate for 1–2 hours for deeper flavor.
3. Place seasoned chicken in the air fryer basket and cook for 20–22 minutes, turning/flipping the chicken halfway through cooking.

**Sources and assumptions:**

- Nutrient values come from the USDA National Nutrient Database for Standard Reference, Legacy Release (SR Legacy). It is available in [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/), where each entry can be found by its NDB number. Quantities were converted to grams using USDA portion weights where available.
- USDA entries used: chicken thighs: NDB 05096 (Chicken, broilers or fryers, dark meat, thigh, meat only, raw); olive oil mayonnaise: NDB 04025 (Salad dressing, mayonnaise, regular); thyme: NDB 02042 (Spices, thyme, dried); cumin: NDB 02014 (Spices, cumin seed); oregano: NDB 02027 (Spices, oregano, dried); fennel: NDB 02018 (Spices, fennel seed); salt: NDB 02047 (Salt, table); pepper: NDB 02030 (Spices, pepper, black).
- Uses chicken thighs. With chicken breasts (NDB 05062), calories stay about the same, and each serving has about 5 g more protein and 2.5 g less fat.
- Olive oil mayonnaise is calculated as regular mayonnaise. A reduced-fat olive oil mayo saves less than 10 calories per serving.
- Cumin and fennel use the USDA seed entries, the closest match for the ground spices.
